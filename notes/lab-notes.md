# Lab notes

**Date:** 3 November 2026, kitchen of the institute, morning.

## Setup

- One mug (ceramic, about 300 ml), filled with freshly brewed filter coffee.
- Digital kitchen thermometer, probe in the middle of the mug, about 2 cm below the surface.
- Room thermometer on the shelf: **21.5 °C**, steady over the whole hour.
- No lid, no stirring, no milk. Window closed.

## Procedure

1. Poured the coffee, put the probe in, started the stopwatch at the first reading.
2. Read the temperature every 3 minutes for 60 minutes and wrote it down (see `data/coffee-cooling.csv`).
3. Readings are rounded to 0.1 °C; the probe's specified accuracy is ±0.5 °C.

## Observations

- The surface steamed visibly for the first ~15 minutes.
- A colleague opened the door at about minute 40; no obvious jump in the readings.
- At 60 minutes the coffee felt lukewarm, about right for drinking quickly.

## Question

Does Newton's law of cooling describe this, and when does the coffee reach 60 °C, the usual "drinkable" temperature?
